Very interesting post. Thanks for sharing. And very happy to hear stories of someone relieving themselves of so much suffering.

Based on the QTd post, I’m not sure how welcome reflections on your post are but hopefully my reflections won’t feel simplistic or heavy handed.

I’m biased by having a Buddhist outlook with a Mahayana orientation, so this may not be relevant so someone who doesn’t.

But I find in my own path that the journey of alleviating suffering is, at least in this life, to all intents and purposes endless.

One of the insights necessary to releasing a lot of our fundamental suffering knots is seeing that our sense of self is an illusion.

So the idea of some suffering belonging to us and other suffering belonging to others disappears.

Because of the vast oceans of suffering out there in the world, there is always more suffering to be addressed, whether or not it fits within the arbitrary container of our illusory self.

As the suffering in our own perceived bodymind diminishes, we become more and more available to service the suffering present in what we perceive as “other people.” 

So, the path becomes increasingly about compassion, which can hopefully arise increasingly spontaneously and give us a compass that replaces our now redundant compass of shaping the external world into conditions that alleviate our now (largely alleviated) suffering.

Beyond the compassion angle, I can say that in my own journey, I often find that my currently favourable conditions (no severe chronic or terminal illness, the full effects of aging yet to be felt, my basic physical needs met) can at times give me an illusory sense of how much suffering I’ve yet conquered.

To me, the suffering that arises from reactivity to traumas and day to day life etc is a largely psychological matter so (relatively!) easier to make headyway on quite rapidly.

The suffering attached to birth, old age terminal or severe chronic sickness and death often has deeper roots that can only be truly uprooted by a complete insight into our true nature beyond our perceived identity as an individual human.

In my own journey, I tend to find that even when psychological suffering is largely quelled, there’s still deep oceans to explore in these more spiritual, metaphysical territories. And the serene conditions offered by psychological calm offer a rare opportunity to dive deep into these oceans and address knots that lie beyond this individual lifetime.

So, in a nutshell, once psychological suffering and reactivity to physical pain diminishes, rich veins I’ve found to explore have been compassion and deeper journeys of transpersonal spiritual insight.

These are the directions that have arisen in my journey and I have no idea if they are relevant to yours. But thought I’d share as it’s a topic I think on regularly (and admittedly your post felt like a good jumping off point for me to get some of my ideas on these matters down on paper for my own benefit too).
