Pretty much describes the process of "awakening" I've experienced myself.

First clearing layers of conditioning through introspection, attention, and emotional resolution. Analyzing situations, understanding different factors, people, and environment. Sitting with the emotion and seeing through to the end. Accepting it. Letting it go. Dying a little psychological death as a part of me that made up a rigid sense of identity was let go.
Rinse and repeat, with increasingly deeper levels of nuances, subtlety, and relying less and less on words and mental narratives, just felt experience.

That cleared the actual physical lens of the way I perceived the world.
Same experience with brighter colors, more vive life, more expansive awareness, to this day it hasn't changed.

Then the flashes of memories and past stuff that comes up is similar too.
But there is no longer any resistance or attachment.
I think it's just the regular way of the mind/body to process life experiences. Like ashes that keep burning and stay hot for a while after the fire is gone.

Since life doesn't stop, you keep accumulating positive and negative experience, and they all need processing. Even after "enlightenment" whatever that means, this doesn't stop. Just that the process becomes quicker and quicker. Things just don't really "stick".

From there, I think it's about keeping up with moments of inquiry, and pushing it deeper, in more subtle conditionings. And it's about cultivating that state of non-duality/potentiality in every moment of the day, increasingly while living life, until it becomes a default state.

Though, as you said, the rigidity of modern existence, with past, present, future, plans, goals, rigid systems and strucures, makes it easy to fall back into "living inside your mind".
Ultimately, it's just a matter of not identifying with any of it, and keeping that fluidity in the moment to moment experience, no matter if attention is in the mind, in the body, or in the world. No real difference from the absolute perspective.
