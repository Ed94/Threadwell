Intelligence is not just the ability to reason, but the ability to spend reasoning resources wisely. Attention, memory, time, and computation are limited, so an intelligent system must identify what matters for the current goal, compress it into a useful abstraction, and keep irrelevant detail outside active attention.

But relevance is contextual, compression is lossy, and ignored details may later prove decisive. The goal cannot be deletion. A robust system needs layered representations: a compact working model, links to supporting evidence, and the ability to recover omitted information when confidence drops, contradictions appear, risks rise, or goals change.

This makes intelligence a problem of adaptive resource allocation and metacognition: deciding what to think about, at what level of detail, for how long, and when to reconsider. The core tension is efficiency versus fidelity, speed versus robustness, and compression versus recoverability. Intelligence lies in managing these trade-offs dynamically.
