This is a minor pet peeve of mine, because the quoted tweet is wrong on both accounts.

1) The Vatican didn't "admit" anything in 1992. The general ban on heliocentric books ended in 1758, and JPII's speech blamed theologians for confusing Scripture's interpretation with science. He made no astronomical ruling.

2) Galileo had not shown he was right. Tycho's Earth-centred model (geoheliocentrism) fit the observations of the day, and stellar parallax wasn't observed until 1838. Ironically, Galileo's own relativity argument said no experiment on Earth could reveal its motion, so he leaned on the tides as proof, and that was wrong. Galileo was a brilliant astronomer and a pioneer of many things, but he claimed more certainty than his evidence allowed.

The Church allowed Copernicus's model as a hypothesis of calculation (1620 decree). But what it forbade was asserting that hypothesis as fact, which Galileo was judged to have done despite a 1616 order. Hence his "palace arrest": a Medici villa, an archbishop's palace, then his own villa with servants. Galileo was a well-paid Medici courtier and was never put in a cell.
