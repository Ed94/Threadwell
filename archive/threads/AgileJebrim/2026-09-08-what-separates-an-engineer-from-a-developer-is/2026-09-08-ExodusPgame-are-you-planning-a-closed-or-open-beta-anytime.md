**2/** **@AgileJebrim** ^2097331912138187180

No. We’re still making breaking changes at this time. Once we put something out there, the interface will basically be locked in place. We want to ensure we get it right.

Furthermore, since we care so much about rigorous guarantees, we’re going to be testing this to death long before any user actually sees anything. I don’t think it helps reputation-wise to release something that fails to deliver on its promises. I have no desire to release broken software, which is generally what betas are for.

I currently do plan on offering free GPU-interpreted scripting in a browser-like environment, akin to Shadertoy, Compute Toys, and FragCoord, but as a native application that is able to have proper I/O. You’ll get a fraction of the throughput performance of a compiled application, won’t own the code, and the source must be made publicly available, but otherwise you’ll get to try out most of the capabilities to see if it’s right for you.

My guess is that it’ll be available sometime in 2028. There’s still a lot of work to do.
