**1/** **@EricLengyel** ^1781130015209574593

This is a few years old, but holy crap, I can't believe a peer-reviewed journal actually published a paper claiming to have found an 8D associative division algebra. This was proven impossible over 100 years ago. (It follows from Hurwitz's theorem.) 1/4
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2020/09/new_normed_division_algebra_fo.html

**2/** **@EricLengyel** ^1781130016480547027

The paper claims that the 8D division algebra is the even part of the geometric algebra Cl(4,0). Any qualified reviewer would have known that the even part of Cl(4,0) has zero divisors (like 1 + e₁₂₃₄), instantly proving the paper wrong, but it somehow still got through! 2/4

**3/** **@EricLengyel** ^1781130017579389343

This is one more example of how the value of peer review has been taking a nose dive. Nowadays, if something is peer reviewed, it doesn't necessarily mean it's any good at all. (Conversely, if something is not peer reviewed, it doesn't mean it's of questionable quality.) 3/4

**4/** **@EricLengyel** ^1781130018732888239

This paper did end up getting retracted, but it must have been very embarrassing for the journal. 4/4
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2020/10/no_new_normed_division_algebra.html
