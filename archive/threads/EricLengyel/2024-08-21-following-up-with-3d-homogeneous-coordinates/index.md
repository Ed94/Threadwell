If you think of the Hodge star of the vector, then the vector turns to a bivector which represents a line. The bivector turns to a vector with the Hodge star which represents a point.

**3/** **@EricLengyel** ^1826168886666080574

The Hodge star does not do what you think it does in this case due to the degenerate metric. But the complement does. You are correct that the red objects correspond to the subspace orthogonal to the one occupied by the blue vector or bivector.
