Rotations in 3D space have 3 degrees of freedom. This means they *can* be unambiguously represented by 3 numbers. Since a quaternion q corresponds to the same rotation as −q, we can always choose one with unit magnitude such that the scalar part (w) is positive. 1/2

**2/** **@EricLengyel** ^1482868184617152513

**@joonturbo**

Any rotation is then unambiguously represented by only the x, y, and z coordinates of the bivector part because these determine that value of the scalar part as w = sqrt(1 − x² − y² − z²).
