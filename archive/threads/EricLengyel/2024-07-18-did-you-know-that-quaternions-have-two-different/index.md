Did you know that quaternions have two different representations in homogeneous coordinates? They make up the special orthogonal group SO(3), but that's a subgroup of two larger complementary transformation groups.

First, quaternions are part of the special Euclidean group SE(3), where they correspond to rotations about the origin. Second, quaternions are part of the special complement Euclidean group SE̅(3), where they correspond to motions of constant eccentricity with the directrix in the horizon, which are just rotations about the origin again. SO(3) is the intersection of SE(3) and SE̅(3).
