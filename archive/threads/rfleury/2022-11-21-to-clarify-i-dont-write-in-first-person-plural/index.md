To clarify, I don’t write in first person plural for a reason—I don’t believe in “we”, and I don’t care to enforce my preferences on others. The proof is really in the pudding. It’s not within the realm of feasibility (nor perhaps desirability) to “decide the issue” universally.

**2/** **@rfleury** ^1594748740103208960

**@AllenWebster4th** **@MemoryArenaMain**

In my opinion it *is* within reason to teach/convince *enough* people that the simplification offered by arenas is compelling, such that a growing population finds themselves improved as programmers, and decide to adopt the strategy in their own work.

**3/** **@rfleury** ^1594749526510034945

**@AllenWebster4th** **@MemoryArenaMain**

I find arenas an incredibly solid default allocation abstraction for a wide array of use cases. I don’t know what would be more convincing to others than a massive world of software built with this in mind, serving as empirical evidence of improved techniques.
