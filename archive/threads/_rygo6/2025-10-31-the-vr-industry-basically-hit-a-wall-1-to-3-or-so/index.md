The VR industry basically hit a wall 1 to 3 or so years ago due to it becoming clear that no VR effort has thus far hit the scale that any higher-up wanted. I'd say that applies to every company. 

The Apple Vision Pro releasing to something less than the iPhone moment of VR was also a hit against. As it created the sentiment, if Apple can't do it with all their resource, then who can? 

Pair that with every higher-up decision-maker in any given company tending to hate HMDs because they are prone to nausea. I suspect there is an association with age and how prone to nausea someone is in VR. 

Several years ago every decision maker at the top I could think of seemed to unanimously decide they're never going to put on an HMD again. I suspect it's because at one point it made them terribly nauseous and they just aren't willing to put in the time to develop VR legs. So they have been sitting there low-key hating VR which they see as a nausea machine they just don't "get", cannot "get" and are unwilling to "get" because of that. Anytime they may have touched a toe into something like VRChat to look deeper, I'm sure whatever happened was a significant "Wtf" moment.

Such people are supposed to be in a major tech leadership position, but are unable to even regularly use the new hardware which has all the buzz. So there's been a sense of looking for anyway to justify ignoring it. Or just keeping it in the corner with minimal funding until the "real" thing can come. 

There is also a sentiment that VR games have some inherent complication which will always inhibit them from getting mainstream integration comparable to desktop or console. Which is probably true. But this is why investment in new VR game startups have become pretty much impossible with all other investment in general becoming difficult. VR gaming is not seen as a fruitful future by many with capital and decision-making power. 

I'd say this is why the tone has shifted to AR glasses in a lot of the industry. 

There has been no new technology in VR optics made available which is significant enough to change the sentiment of those who loathe VR. 

Whereas see-through AR optics have gone through a few generations of advancement which have not seen the consumer space in a number of years and could garner interest. I'd suspect that new see-through AR optics are the only tech you could have approached higher up decision makers with in the past few years or so, then get them to actually put on and buy into. Then allocate the big budget and let through the bureaucracy of release. 

Android XR making it out actually surprised me. I suspect that gathered momentum with the Apple Vision Pro announcement and since the ball already rolled so far, might as well let it out the door. Google tends to be the most lax in releasing things.

This is why, if you asked me, I would put more bets on if anything does come from Valve it is AR glasses. It's the only thing you could rally support for among those industry-wide who have been low-key hating VR in the background.

Which a pair of AR glasses with appropriate field of view and nice sound built in, with good UX, very lightweight and comfortable, done right. I would probably use that if it provided a decent virtual screen experience in a dark room. 

I could be completely wrong and this will be an embarrassing prediction in however long. In the least I'd expect it to be very non-immersive-VR content focused. Very much more so sit-and-use-as-a-big-screen focused as some kind of extension to the steamdeck. I will be marginally more surprised than not if it does not have see-through AR optics given the sentiment on VR across the industry in recent years. 

It still is kind of sad industry-wide to me. As everyone who hated VR could of just let those who really "got" it have more of their own discretion. But that's not how corporate social politics works. You either got to be the centre of everything or shun it. 

To which, a well done pair of AR glasses could find a niche committed user base. But that won't be the iPhone moment of XR either. Hopefully, maybe, after this cycle of XR hype, those whom have been deep in VR for many years now will be able to affect what they need to in order to take it where it needs to go in another several years or so. The real metaverse. Before everyone redefined it to something simpler to get VC funding.
