Looking for an “iPhone moment” in XR is a major logical fallacy. The iPhone benefitted from a unique convergence — cell phones were already universal, and smartphones had existed for years. The iPhone was simply a better version of something people already used daily.

Spatial computing, on the other hand, represents a new computing medium — its adoption cycle is far more akin to that of the personal computer than the smartphone. Expecting instant mainstream uptake misunderstands both history and behavior.

As for Android XR, this isn’t a casual experiment. It’s Google’s second (third if you count Cardboard) major effort (Daydream) — a clear signal of a deliberate, long-term strategy rather than a reactionary one.
