The reason fancy abstraction and all these advanced language features are so popular, are so talked about and consequently develop this aura of being so important due to all the social attention they garner. It's because it is easier to fiddle with language features than it is to program something which does anything useful. It gives people a way to appear very knowledgeable about programming without actually programming anything of value. So many could talk ad nauseam about architectural concepts and the philosophy of certain language features but then struggle to come up with a set of bespoke data structures optimally designed and implemented for their specific use case. Or struggle to interface directly with an OS header file or graphics driver header file in a way optimal for their use case.

This is the reason. It lets you fill your mind with a bunch of easier stuff to create the perception of expansive knowledge with less work.

This has really been a hot ticket the past few decades or so. Particularly riding on the back of the rise of Java and everything which came from that. Making the industry believe fancy enterprise architectural concepts were the big thing to solve all the problems. There was a mass gold rush of a rapidly expanding industry and companies needed lots of developers. The quickest way to fill your mind, blog and books with lots of stuff to appear like you have expansive and complex knowledge was to churn through all this simpler surface level stuff. Design Patterns.  Principles. Enterprise Architecture. Stylistic Best Practices. etc. etc. You could be the person with a handful of cryptic native low-level applications solving difficult deep problems on your website that only a small percentage of the most technically advanced would know or care about. Or you could be the dude with a blog and lectures containing a bunch of high-level more easily grokkable concepts that have lots of buzz in discussion and even non-technical people could be amused by. Being the latter was definitely easier to attract attention and get others to perceive as having expansive knowledge about programming. There was actual economic incentive to be the latter.

I believe the industry is changing. You no longer want to be the latter. You no longer want to be in the mass arguing high level philosophical concepts and writing blogs about theory.

You want to be the person who has some minimal library on your github that solves some real practical problem better than anything else. Not an argument or theory for anything, but something other people are downloading and actively using, demonstrating you know how to identify gaps in the industry that truly need to be solved and you can solve actual problems that programmers need solved better than what anyone else has done.
